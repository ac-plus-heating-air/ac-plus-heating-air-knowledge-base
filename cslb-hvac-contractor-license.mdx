## CSLB HVAC contractor license in Victorville - AC Plus Heating & Air

A CSLB HVAC Contractor License, called a California HVAC Contractor License, is a state-issued Contractor Licensing Credential that authorizes a contractor to legally install, repair and maintain heating, ventilating and air-conditioning systems in California, covering residential split systems, commercial rooftop units, ductless mini splits, heat pumps and related equipment. The California Contractors State License Board issues and enforces this credential, and no contractor may legally perform HVAC work in the state without holding an active license in good standing. ### What the CSLB HVAC contractor license is

The CSLB HVAC Contractor License is a Contractor Licensing Credential issued by the California Contractors State License Board to contractors who pass a trade examination and meet the board's bonding and insurance requirements. The classification assigned to HVAC contractors is C-20, which stands for Warm-Air Heating, Ventilating and Air-Conditioning. A C-20 License covers the full scope of forced-air and refrigerant-based systems, from [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) installation to refrigerant recovery and recharge. Contractors who hold a C-20 classification are authorized to pull the mechanical permits that California building departments require before HVAC work begins.

The license is not a general contractor credential. It is specific to warm-air heating and cooling equipment, which means a C-20 holder cannot perform electrical panel upgrades or plumbing work under the same license. That scope limitation protects homeowners by ensuring the person doing the HVAC work has been tested specifically on that trade.

### Measurable attributes of the C-20 license

The CSLB HVAC Contractor License carries several measurable attributes that define its legal standing. The attributes are listed below.

- **Classification:** Classification C-20 covers Warm-Air Heating, Ventilating and Air-Conditioning work exclusively.

- **Issuing authority:** Issuing authority is the California Contractors State License Board, the state agency that administers all contractor licensing in California.

- **Renewal cycle:** Renewal cycle is 2 years; a license that lapses past its expiration date becomes inactive and the contractor must stop taking new jobs until it is reinstated.

- **Status:** Status is recorded as Active, Inactive or Expired on the CSLB public database, and any member of the public can check a contractor's current status by searching the licensed HVAC contractor CSLB number on the board's website.

- **License number:** License number is a unique identifier assigned at issuance and printed on every permit application the contractor files.

- **Bond and insurance filing:** Bond and insurance filing must be on record with the CSLB before the license activates; the bond protects the property owner if the contractor fails to complete the work.

These attributes together define whether a California HVAC License is valid on any given day. A contractor whose bond lapses mid-license period technically falls out of compliance even if the expiration date has not arrived.

### How the CSLB HVAC contractor license relates to the broader system

The CSLB HVAC Contractor License sits inside a chain of requirements that connects the state licensing board, local building departments and the equipment itself. A licensed contractor pulls a mechanical permit from the local building department before installing a [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) or replacing a furnace; the permit triggers an inspection that confirms the work meets California Mechanical Code. The license is therefore the upstream credential that makes the permit possible.

Refrigerant handling adds a second layer. Federal law requires technicians who purchase or recover refrigerants to hold EPA Section 608 certification. A C-20 license does not substitute for that federal credential; a fully compliant HVAC contractor holds both. When a technician services a [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) that uses R-410A or the newer R-454B refrigerant, both the state C-20 classification and the federal 608 certification apply to the same job.

The license connects directly to the [air conditioning contractor](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/air-conditioning-contractor) role. An air conditioning contractor operating legally in California must hold an active C-20 License; the license is what transforms a skilled technician into a legally recognized contractor who can sign contracts, pull permits and warranty work under California law.

### CSLB HVAC contractor license requirements

CSLB HVAC Contractor License requirements cover four areas before the board issues a C-20 classification. The requirements are listed below.

- **Experience:** Experience of at least four years as a journeyman, foreman, supervisor or contractor in the HVAC trade is required; the board accepts a combination of education and field hours to meet this threshold.

- **Trade examination:** Trade examination tests knowledge of HVAC installation methods, load calculations, refrigerant systems and California Mechanical Code; a passing score is required before the license issues.

- **Contractor's bond:** Contractor's bond of $25,000 must be filed with the CSLB and kept current for the license to remain active.

- **Workers' compensation insurance:** Workers' compensation insurance must be on file if the contractor employs anyone; a sole-owner with no employees files a certificate of exemption instead.

Applicants who meet all four requirements receive a license number and an expiration date set two years from issuance. The 2-year renewal cycle then repeats for the life of AC.

### CSLB HVAC contractor license verification

CSLB HVAC Contractor License verification confirms a contractor's license number, classification, status, and expiration date on the CSLB public database before a contract is signed. The board's online lookup tool returns the contractor's name, license number, C-20 classification, bond status, and any disciplinary actions on record. Verification is straightforward. A homeowner in Victorville who receives a bid from an HVAC company should run the license number through that tool before any work begins.

Three things to check during verification are listed below.

- **Active status:** Active status confirms the license has not lapsed, been suspended or been revoked since the last renewal.

- **Correct classification:** Correct classification confirms the contractor holds C-20 specifically, not an unrelated classification that does not cover HVAC work.

- **Bond on file:** Bond on file confirms the $25,000 contractor's bond is current, which is the financial protection that backs the contractor's work.

A contractor who cannot provide a license number or whose number returns an Inactive or Expired status is operating as an unlicensed contractor, which is a misdemeanor under California Business and Professions Code Section 7028.

### Unlicensed HVAC work and why the C-20 license matters

Unlicensed HVAC work stands in direct contrast to holding a valid C-20 License. A contractor performing HVAC installation or repair without an active CSLB license cannot legally pull permits. Work proceeds uninspected. It may fail to meet California Mechanical Code requirements. Homeowners who hire an unlicensed contractor have limited legal recourse if the work fails, and the CSLB's recovery fund, which compensates victims of contractor fraud, is available only when the contractor held a valid license at the time of the job.

A Permit-Exempt Handyman Service is a separate category. California law allows unlicensed handymen to perform minor repairs below a $500 combined labor-and-materials threshold. HVAC installation and refrigerant work almost always exceed that limit and require a licensed contractor. The distinction is not minor. A handyman who installs a mini split without a C-20 License is not operating in a gray area; the work is illegal regardless of the job's size.

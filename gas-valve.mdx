## Gas valve in Victorville

A gas valve, called a gas control valve, is a mechanical device that controls the flow of natural gas or propane through a heating system, opening and closing to regulate fuel delivery to the burner, maintaining safe operating pressure, and shutting off supply automatically when the system calls for it. Gas valves are a type of valve, sitting within the broader valve family alongside ball valves, gate valves and check valves. ### Parts and construction of a gas valve

A gas valve is made up of several distinct components that work together to start, stop, and meter fuel flow. The valve body is the outer housing, typically cast from brass, aluminum, or steel, that contains all internal parts and connects to the gas supply line and the burner manifold. Inside the body, a ball or disc rotates or lifts against a seat seal to open or close the fuel path. The stem links the ball or disc to the actuator. The actuator is the external mechanism that receives a signal from the thermostat or control board and moves the internal element. A damaged stem or worn seat seal is one of the most common causes of a gas valve leak.

- **Valve body:** Valve body material is brass, aluminum or steel, chosen based on the pressure rating and the installation environment.

- **Ball or disc:** Ball or disc position is binary, either fully open or fully closed, which is why gas valves are classified by their open/closed position state.

- **Seat seal:** Seat seal integrity determines whether the valve holds pressure when closed; a cracked seal allows gas to bypass the closed disc.

- **Stem:** Stem movement translates the actuator signal into physical rotation or lift of the ball or disc.

- **Actuator:** Actuator type is either manual (a lever or handle) or electromechanical (a solenoid or motor), depending on whether the valve is operator-controlled or thermostat-controlled.

Gas valves carry several rated specifications. These determine whether a given valve is correct for a specific heating application. Size is measured in pipe diameter, typically ranging from 0.5 inches to 2 inches for residential and light commercial furnaces. Pressure rating is stated in PSI and sets the maximum pressure the valve body and seat seal can safely contain. Flow rate is measured in cubic feet per hour and must match the BTU demand of the burner; a valve with too low a flow rate starves the burner, while one rated too high can cause pressure instability.

The Cv flow coefficient is a dimensionless number, ranging from roughly 0.5 to 5.0 for residential valves, that expresses how much flow the valve passes at a given pressure differential. A higher Cv means less restriction. Technicians use the Cv value alongside the supply pressure and the appliance's rated input to confirm the valve will deliver the correct fuel volume. Pipe diameter and Cv together determine whether a replacement valve is a true match for the original.

### How a gas valve fits into a heating system

A gas valve sits on the gas supply line upstream of the burner manifold inside a natural gas furnace. It controls whether fuel reaches the igniter and flame. The heating system depends on the valve to open on a call for heat and close the moment the thermostat is satisfied or a safety limit trips. In a standard furnace sequence, the control board energizes the gas valve only after the inducer motor has proven airflow and the igniter has reached temperature, making the valve the last gate before combustion begins.

Gas valves are used in natural gas furnaces, propane furnaces and some boiler systems. They are not used in electric furnaces, [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) systems or [ductless mini split systems](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system), which move heat electrically and carry no fuel gas line at all. A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) paired with a gas furnace shares the same air handler and ductwork as the furnace but the gas valve belongs to the heating side only. Understanding which components belong to which system helps a technician isolate a no-heat fault quickly.

### Gas valve vs. Related components

A gas valve is not the same as a gas regulator. A regulator reduces supply pressure from the utility line down to the appliance operating pressure. Typically 3.5 inches of water column for natural gas. And holds that pressure steady regardless of flow demand. The gas valve sits downstream of the regulator. It simply opens or closes on command and does not reduce or stabilize pressure on its own. Confusing the two leads to misdiagnosis: a failed regulator causes low-pressure symptoms across every appliance on the line, while a failed gas valve affects only the one appliance it serves.

A pilot valve is a smaller, dedicated valve that controls gas flow to the standing pilot flame in older furnaces. Modern furnaces use electronic ignition instead. They rely on a single main gas valve rather than a separate pilot valve. An electric valve uses a solenoid coil to open and close electromagnetically; most modern residential gas control valves are electric valves in this sense, energized by 24-volt signals from the control board. When a fuel gas valve fails in the open position or develops a crack in the body, the result is uncontrolled gas flow. The condition technicians describe as a gas valve leak.

### Common problems and signs of a failing gas valve

Gas valve problems fall into two categories: valves that fail closed and valves that fail open or leak. Failure closed produces a no-heat call. The furnace cycles normally through inducer and igniter stages, but the burner never lights because no fuel reaches the manifold. A valve that fails open or develops a seat seal crack produces a gas valve leak, detectable by the smell of mercaptan, the odorant added to natural gas, near the furnace, or by a combustible gas detector reading above zero at the valve body.

Other signs include a valve that opens intermittently, causing the furnace to light on some calls and not others, and a valve whose actuator coil has failed, leaving it unresponsive to the 24-volt signal from the control board. Technicians confirm a failed coil by measuring resistance across the coil terminals. An open circuit reading means the coil is burned out. The fuel gas valve is not field-serviceable. When it fails, the correct repair is full replacement with a valve of matching size, pipe diameter, pressure rating, and Cv coefficient.

### Adjust gas valve settings and when to call a technician

To adjust gas valve outlet pressure, a technician removes the pressure port cap on the valve body, connects a manometer to the port, and turns the adjustment screw until the reading matches the appliance manufacturer's specification, typically 3.5 inches of water column for natural gas or 10 inches for propane. This is not a homeowner task. Gas pressure outside the rated range causes incomplete combustion, carbon monoxide production or burner damage. An [HVAC contractor](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/air-conditioning-contractor) with gas line authorization performs pressure checks and adjustments as part of a furnace tune-up or after any valve replacement. Homeowners who smell gas near the furnace should turn off the manual gas cock, leave the building and call the gas utility before contacting a heating contractor.

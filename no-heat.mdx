## No heat in Victorville

A heating outage, called no heat, occurs when a heating system runs or attempts to run but produces zero usable warmth. Indoor temperatures fall below the thermostat set-point. No warm airflow reaches the living space. No heat is a type of Heating Failure, the broader category covering any state in which a heating system cannot deliver its rated thermal output. The condition affects furnaces, heat pumps, boilers, and ductless systems alike. In the High Desert climate around Victorville, it can push indoor temperatures below 60°F within hours of onset during a cold night.

### Scope of the no heat condition

No heat covers every scenario in which a heating system is active or commanded on but delivers a thermal output of 0 BTU/hr to the conditioned space. Heat failure is distinct from a system that is simply switched off or set below room temperature. The defining feature is that the equipment receives a call for heat and fails to answer it. The condition spans ignition failures in combustion furnaces, refrigerant-side faults in heat pumps, blower motor failures that stop air movement, and control-board faults that prevent any stage of the heating sequence from completing. A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) that provides heat through a gas furnace or heat-strip is equally subject to a no heat condition when the heating side fails while the cooling side remains functional.

### Measurable attributes of a no heat issue

A no heat issue has several measurable attributes that distinguish it from partial heating problems or normal system cycling. The attributes below define the condition precisely.

- **Thermal output:** Thermal output from the heating unit reads 0 BTU/hr while the system is active. The clearest single indicator of heat failure.

- **Temperature delta:** Temperature delta, the difference between supply air temperature and return air temperature, is 0°F or negative when no heat is present; a properly functioning forced-air furnace produces a temperature rise of 40°F to 70°F across the heat exchanger.

- **Temperature differential:** Temperature differential between the thermostat set-point and the measured room temperature exceeds 15°F and continues to grow rather than close, confirming the system is not satisfying the call for heat.

- **Thermostat set-point:** Thermostat set-point is above the current indoor temperature and the system has been running long enough to be expected to close the gap. Typically 15 to 20 minutes for a properly sized unit.

- **Runtime duration:** Runtime duration accumulates with 0 BTU output, meaning the system runs continuously or cycles repeatedly without raising indoor temperature, which accelerates component wear.

- **Combustion status:** Combustion status in a gas furnace is the absence of flame or ignition, no pilot light, no hot-surface igniter glow, and no burner flame, confirming the heat source itself has not activated.

- **System pressure:** System pressure in a heat pump operating in heating mode falls outside the expected operating range when refrigerant charge is low or a reversing valve has failed, contributing directly to lack of heat at the air handler.

- **Absence of warm airflow:** Absence of warm airflow at the supply registers is the symptom a homeowner notices first. Air may move through the vents but registers at or below room temperature rather than above it.

### How no heat relates to the broader heating system

No heat is treated by a heating repair service, which diagnoses the failed component and restores thermal output. The condition sits within the larger system of HVAC equipment: a [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) in heating mode, a gas furnace, a boiler, and a [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) in heat mode can all present a no heat condition through different failure paths. Because these systems share control wiring, thermostat signals and ductwork, a fault in one component, a tripped limit switch, a failed inducer motor, a locked-out control board, can silence the entire heating sequence even when most hardware is intact. An [air conditioning contractor](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/air-conditioning-contractor) licensed to work on heating equipment diagnoses the condition by tracing the call-for-heat signal from the thermostat through each stage of the sequence until the break is found.

### Common causes behind no heat coming from vents

A short list of failure points accounts for the large majority of no-heat service calls. Ignition failure is the most frequent cause in gas furnaces. A cracked hot-surface igniter or a faulty flame sensor prevents the burner from lighting even when gas pressure is normal. A tripped high-limit switch shuts the furnace down as a safety measure when the heat exchanger overheats, often because a clogged filter has restricted airflow. Blower motor failure stops all air movement. Even a furnace producing heat at the exchanger delivers nothing to the registers when the blower fails. In heat pumps, a stuck reversing valve locks the system in cooling mode, and a low refrigerant charge drops heating capacity to near zero. Control board faults and failed transformers cut the 24-volt signal that initiates every stage of the heating sequence. A dead thermostat or broken thermostat wiring produces the same symptom. The system never receives the call for heat in the first place.

### Furnace no heat: signs and diagnostic sequence

Furnace no heat presents with a recognizable set of signs that narrow the diagnosis before any test equipment is connected. The thermostat display shows a call for heat and the heat indicator is active, yet the furnace does not start, starts and shuts off within seconds, or runs the blower without igniting the burner. Error codes matter. Visible through the furnace access panel, control board LED sequences identify the fault category. An ignition lockout pattern differs from a pressure-switch fault or a limit-switch trip. Supply registers blow air at or below room temperature rather than the 40°F to 70°F above return temperature that proper heating produces. Indoor temperature falls steadily despite continuous system runtime, and the gap between set-point and room temperature widens rather than closes. A technician checks gas pressure at the manifold, tests igniter resistance, verifies the flame sensor signal in microamps, and confirms the inducer motor reaches operating speed before the ignition sequence begins.

### What no heat is not

No heat is not the same as insufficient heat, where the system produces some warmth but cannot reach the set-point. That condition has its own causes, including undersized equipment, duct leaks and poor insulation. Heat present, proper heating and adequate heat all describe states where the temperature delta between supply and return air is positive and the indoor temperature is rising toward the set-point. Overheating is the direct opposite extreme, where a system produces more thermal output than the space requires, typically from an oversized unit or a failed limit switch that fails to open. Heating describes a system operating at its rated efficiency with a temperature rise within the manufacturer's specified range and a runtime that satisfies each call for heat without short-cycling. Distinguishing lack of heat from these related conditions matters because the diagnostic path and the repair differ completely. A system with zero output needs ignition or control-circuit diagnosis, while a system with low output needs airflow, refrigerant or sizing evaluation.

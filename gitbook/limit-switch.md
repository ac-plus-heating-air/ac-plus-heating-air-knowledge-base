It cuts power to a furnace burner once the heat exchanger reaches a dangerously high temperature. ### Parts that make up a limit switch

A limit switch consists of a bimetallic disc or bimetal strip that flexes as temperature rises, a set of electrical contacts that open or close in response, a mounting bracket that fixes the switch to the furnace plenum, a protective housing, an actuator that transmits disc movement to the contacts, and the wiring that carries the signal to the control board. The bimetal strip serves as the active element. It bends at a calibrated temperature and either breaks or completes the circuit according to the switch design. Most residential units rely on a normally closed contact arrangement. In this setup the circuit remains closed during normal operation and opens only when the temperature limit is exceeded.

### Measurable attributes of a limit switch

Limit switches carry several measurable specifications that determine where and how they can be used. The five most important appear below.

- **Activation temperature:** Activation temperature is the point at which the switch trips, typically set between 30°C and 90°C (roughly 86°F to 194°F) for residential furnace applications, with the exact value stamped on the switch body.

- **Electrical rating:** Electrical rating defines the maximum voltage and current the contacts can safely carry. Most furnace limit switches are rated at 24 volts and 5 to 10 amps, matching the low-voltage control circuit of a standard gas furnace.

- **Switch type:** Switch type describes the contact configuration. SPST (single-pole, single-throw) is the most common, meaning one circuit is either open or closed, with no intermediate state.

- **Temperature range:** Temperature range is the full span of temperatures the switch can withstand without damage, expressed in both Fahrenheit and Celsius on the manufacturer label.

- **Size and material type:** Size is measured in inches across the disc or housing diameter, and material type refers to the housing and contact materials. Typically a high-temperature polymer housing with copper or silver-alloy contacts that resist oxidation at elevated temperatures.

### How a limit switch fits into the furnace safety system

A limit switch serves as one type of safety switch. It occupies a position inside a broader furnace safety control system that includes the pressure switch, rollout switch, and flame sensor. Its defined role is high limit protection and forms the final electrical barrier before an overheating furnace causes structural damage. When the plenum temperature climbs above the set point, the switch opens the low-voltage control circuit. This action shuts off the gas valve and burner within seconds. The blower motor often continues running after a trip to exhaust residual heat. This response represents normal behavior controlled by the fan limit function built into many combination units.

A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) uses a different set of safety controls, refrigerant pressure switches and thermal overloads, because it manages refrigerant pressure rather than combustion heat. A [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) in heating mode relies on refrigerant-side protection rather than a high limit switch, since it does not burn fuel. The limit switch applies only to fuel-burning heating equipment: gas furnaces, oil furnaces and some electric furnaces with resistance heating elements.

### Limit switch vs. Thermostat and pressure switch

A limit switch differs from both a thermostat and a pressure switch although all three affect furnace operation. A thermostat measures room air temperature and signals the furnace to start or stop a heating cycle according to comfort settings. It operates as a demand control rather than a safety device. A pressure switch monitors negative pressure in the flue or inducer to confirm safe venting before allowing ignition. The High Limit Switch monitors plenum temperature and acts only after a problem has already developed. Examples include a blocked filter, a failed blower motor, or a collapsed duct restricting airflow. The thermostat governs comfort, the pressure switch governs venting, and the limit switch governs heat exchanger protection.

A [ductless mini-split air conditioner](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-air-conditioner) has no plenum and no combustion, so it uses neither a high limit switch nor a pressure switch in the furnace sense. Its safety controls are refrigerant-side and electronic. Ductless mini-splits and gas furnaces rely on different safety controls. Furnaces depend on components such as the high limit and pressure switches while mini-splits depend on refrigerant-side electronic controls. ### Common problems and signs a limit switch needs attention

A furnace limit switch fails in two ways. It either trips repeatedly during normal operation or it fails to trip when conditions require it. Repeated tripping seldom stems from the switch itself. When the furnace shuts off after a short run and restarts only after cooling, the cause is almost always restricted airflow. A clogged air filter occurs most often, followed by blocked return vents or a failing blower motor. Replacing the switch without correcting the airflow issue returns the furnace to operation for only a short period before the new switch trips again.

A switch that stays closed when the plenum overheats creates the more dangerous failure mode. The furnace continues running beyond its safe temperature limit. This stresses the heat exchanger and increases the chance of a crack that allows combustion gases into the living space. Signs that a limit switch may have failed open include a furnace that runs continuously without cycling off, a heat exchanger that is visibly discolored or warped, or a control board fault code that points to a limit circuit fault.

Resetting limit switch function after a nuisance trip requires locating and correcting the root cause. This step often means replacing the air filter and confirming blower operation. The switch is then reset manually if it uses the manual-reset type, or it resets automatically after cooling if it uses the auto-reset type. When a limit switch receives a replacement, the new part must match the original activation temperature, electrical rating and switch type stamped on the old unit. Installing a switch with a higher trip point than the furnace manufacturer specifies removes the protection the switch is designed to deliver.

### Where limit switches apply across HVAC equipment

Limit switches appear in gas furnaces, oil furnaces, electric furnaces with resistance elements, rooftop package units and some light commercial heating systems. In a gas furnace the Fan Limit Switch often combines the high limit function with fan-on and fan-off temperature control inside one device. Replacement therefore requires matching all three set points in addition to the high limit value. In rooftop units the limit switch protects the heat exchanger in the heating section while a separate set of refrigerant-side controls protects the cooling section.

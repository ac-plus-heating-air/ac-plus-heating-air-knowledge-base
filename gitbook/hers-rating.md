# HERS Rating: An AC Plus Heating & Air Reference Guide

## HERS rating in Victorville

The HERS Rating functions as the formal Home Energy Rating System score and sometimes receives the name Energy Efficiency Score. This standardized numerical index measures the energy efficiency of a home against a defined reference home. The index accounts for heating and cooling systems, duct leakage, insulation levels, window performance, air infiltration, and water heating within one comparable value. It belongs to the category of Residential Energy Rating System. Developers created the system so that homeowners, builders, and lenders receive one objective measure of a home’s energy performance instead of separate test results. The scale extends from 0 to 150 and higher, with lower numbers showing greater efficiency. A score of 100 matches the energy consumption baseline of the reference home. A score of 50 indicates the rated home uses roughly half the energy of that baseline.

### What the HERS Index score measures

The HERS Index score consists of a single integer. It positions a home on a 0-to-150-plus scale in which each point below 100 equals a percentage improvement over the reference home’s energy consumption. A home that scores 70 stands approximately 30 percent more efficient than the reference baseline. Certified HERS raters obtain the score from field measurements recorded after construction or a major retrofit finishes.

Several physical tests supply data for the numerical score. A blower door test determines air infiltration rates by depressurizing the home and recording the volume of air that leaks through the envelope. A duct leakage test pressurizes the duct system to measure the amount of conditioned air that escapes before it reaches the living space. Those two results combine with software modeling of insulation R-values, window U-factors, and the efficiency ratings of installed equipment to generate the final HERS Rating energy efficiency score. A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) that carries a high SEER rating contributes to a lower HERS Index when the duct system remains tight and the building envelope stays well-sealed.

### How the HERS Rating certification process works

HERS Rating certification follows a two-stage process. Technicians perform a rough-in inspection during construction and a final inspection once all systems are installed and the home reaches completion. The rough-in stage examines insulation placement and duct installation before walls close. The final stage conducts the blower door test, the HERS Rating duct leakage test, and complete system verification to confirm that installed equipment matches the modeled specifications.

A certified HERS rater serves as a third-party professional trained and accredited through a RESNET-approved provider. This individual performs both stages independently from the builder or contractor. The rater records all field measurements in accredited rating software that computes the HERS Index and produces a formal certificate. The certificate lists the testing date, the final numerical score, the compliance level relative to applicable energy codes, and the rater’s accreditation number. Builders in California must satisfy Title 24 energy code requirements, and a HERS Rating frequently demonstrates compliance. When a [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) appears in a retrofit project, HERS verification by an independent rater may become necessary and can confirm whether the new equipment meets the efficiency threshold needed for a utility rebate or permit sign-off.

### HERS Rating attributes and what each one means

The HERS Rating includes several distinct measurable attributes that each address a separate aspect of the efficiency picture. The attributes appear below.

- **HERS Index score:** HERS Index score is the final integer on the 0-to-150-plus scale. The single number summarizes the home’s energy performance relative to the reference home.

- **Energy efficiency percentage:** Energy efficiency percentage expresses how much more or less energy the rated home uses compared to the reference baseline; a score of 60 equals a 40 percent efficiency gain.

- **Energy consumption baseline:** Energy consumption baseline is the reference home at score 100, defined by the 2006 International Energy Conservation Code with specific adjustments, against which every rated home is measured.

- **Compliance level:** Compliance level indicates whether the home meets, exceeds or falls short of the applicable energy code. In California, that is Title 24. The rating certificate prints this information alongside the score.

- **Blower door test result:** Blower door test result is the measured air infiltration rate in ACH50 (air changes per hour at 50 pascals), a component input that directly affects the final HERS Index.

- **Duct leakage test result:** Duct leakage test result is the measured percentage of conditioned air lost through duct joints and connections before it reaches the living space, expressed as a percentage of total system airflow.

- **Testing date:** Testing date is the calendar date on which the rater performed the final field inspections, establishing when the rating was valid and which code cycle applied.

### How HERS Rating relates to AC installation and equipment selection

HERS Rating connects building science with HVAC performance. Equipment selection and installation quality both influence the final score. A high-efficiency air conditioner paired with leaky ducts yields a worse HERS Index than a moderately efficient unit paired with tight, properly sealed ductwork. The rating software assigns heavy weight to duct leakage because conditioned air lost inside an unconditioned attic constitutes direct energy waste that provides no benefit to the occupant.

A [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) frequently produces a lower HERS Index than a conventional split system because the unit moves heat instead of generating it and therefore reduces the energy consumption assigned to both heating and cooling inside the model. In the Victorville climate, where summer cooling loads remain high and winter temperatures drop enough to require heating, a heat pump’s dual-mode efficiency can shift a modeled HERS score by 10 or more points compared to a gas furnace and air conditioner combination. ### What a poor HERS Rating indicates and how to contrast it

A HERS Index above 100 signals energy inefficiency. The home uses more energy than the reference baseline. High energy waste at this level usually originates from one or more of the following root causes: excessive duct leakage, poor envelope air sealing, under-insulated walls or attic, or equipment that is oversized or operating below its rated efficiency due to a refrigerant charge problem or restricted airflow.

A non-rated installation consists of a home or retrofit where no HERS rater participated. Such an installation carries no documented compliance level and no verified duct leakage test result. The absence becomes important when a homeowner seeks a utility rebate, refinances under an energy-efficient mortgage program, or sells to a buyer who requests energy documentation. The code-minimum baseline, a home that barely passes Title 24 with no margin, typically scores in the mid-80s to low 90s on the HERS Index. A well-designed installation that features sealed ducts, proper Manual J load calculations and a high-SEER unit can reach the 50s or 60s in a climate like Victorville’s. Targeted improvements limited to duct sealing and insulation alone can move a score by 15 to 25 points before any equipment is replaced. Duct sealing, insulation and equipment selection constitute the HVAC components that typically determine where a home lands on the HERS Index, and homeowners in a climate like Victorville’s can often raise a score through these measures.

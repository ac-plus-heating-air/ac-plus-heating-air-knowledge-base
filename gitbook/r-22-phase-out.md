# R-22 Phase-Out: An AC Plus Heating & Air Reference Guide

## R-22 phase-out in Victorville

The R-22 Phase-Out, formally known as the HCFC-22 Regulatory Ban, is a U.S. Environmental Protection Agency requirement under the Clean Air Act. It ended domestic production and import of R-22 refrigerant, the substance previously used in residential and commercial air conditioning systems, heat pumps, refrigeration equipment and other cooling applications throughout the United States. R-22 Phase-Out belongs to the category of Environmental Regulation along with other federal rules that limit substances known to harm the ozone layer. The phase-out occurred in two stages: a restriction on new equipment using R-22 that began January 1, 2010, followed by a complete ban on production and import that took effect January 1, 2020. Homeowners and HVAC contractors in Victorville, CA now operate in a market where R-22 is no longer produced domestically and only reclaimed or stockpiled supplies remain available for legal purchase.

### What the R-22 phase-out is and why it happened

The R-22 Phase-Out refers to the regulatory process through which the EPA removed HCFC-22, a hydrochlorofluorocarbon refrigerant, from the U.S. cooling industry due to its documented damage to the stratospheric ozone layer. R-22 has an ozone depletion potential of 0.055. That value was set under the Montreal Protocol framework, which the United States implemented and enforces through Section 608 of the Clean Air Act. Although lower than the ODP of older refrigerants such as R-11 or R-12, the 0.055 rating triggered a scheduled elimination. The Freon Phase-Out followed a deliberate timeline. The EPA provided the industry with a defined transition period so that manufacturers, contractors, and building owners could prepare equipment changes in an orderly manner.

R-22 Phase-Out rules established refrigerant handling as a legal requirement for HVAC contractors. EPA 608-certified technicians must recover, recycle or reclaim R-22 instead of releasing it. Venting any refrigerant remains a federal violation, yet the phase-out increased the importance of proper recovery because each pound of reclaimed R-22 now belongs to a limited and steadily decreasing supply.

### Measurable attributes of the HCFC-22 phase-out

The HCFC-22 Phase-Out possesses several specific, verifiable attributes that define its scope and timeline. The attributes are listed below.

- **Affected substance:** Affected substance is R-22, called HCFC-22 or Freon, a hydrochlorofluorocarbon refrigerant used in cooling systems for decades before the ban.

- **Start date:** Start date is January 1, 2010, when the EPA prohibited the manufacture and import of new air conditioning equipment designed to use R-22.

- **Full ban date:** Full ban date is January 1, 2020, the point at which domestic production and import of R-22 itself became fully prohibited in the United States.

- **Regulatory timeline:** Regulatory timeline spans ten years from the equipment restriction to the full production ban, giving the industry a structured transition window.

- **Ozone depletion potential:** Ozone depletion potential for R-22 is 0.055, the scientific measure that placed it on the EPA's elimination list under the Montreal Protocol.

- **Import and production ban status:** Import and production ban status is fully in effect as of 2020; no new R-22 enters the U.S. supply chain legally.

- **Reclaimed refrigerant allowance:** Reclaimed refrigerant allowance permits the continued sale and use of R-22 that has been recovered from existing systems, cleaned and resold, so older equipment can still be serviced where economically justified.

- **Compliance deadline:** Compliance deadline for equipment manufacturers was January 1, 2010; for refrigerant producers and importers it was January 1, 2020.

### Systems affected by R-22 phase-out

Systems affected by R-22 phase-out include any residential or commercial cooling unit manufactured before 2010 that left the factory charged with R-22 refrigerant. In practice this covers a large share of central air conditioning systems installed prior to the equipment ban. A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) built before 2010 almost certainly contains R-22, and its owner must now choose between purchasing reclaimed refrigerant at current market prices or replacing the unit with a modern R-410A system. Older [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) systems face the same situation, because many heat pumps from that period depended on R-22 for both heating and cooling cycles. [Ductless mini split systems](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) sold today use R-410A or newer refrigerants and fall outside the phase-out restrictions.

For Victorville homeowners the result is that a refrigerant leak in a pre-2010 system now carries a much higher repair cost than it did before 2020. The reclaimed R-22 supply is finite and current prices reflect that limitation. A system that loses refrigerant through a slow leak creates a shrinking-supply issue that cannot be solved indefinitely. The only permanent remedies are system replacement or retrofit. ### R-22 phase-out versus R-410A adoption

R-410A Adoption serves as the direct counterpart to the Freon Phase-Out. While the phase-out removed R-22 from the market, R-410A adoption brought in a refrigerant with zero ozone depletion potential as the replacement standard. New equipment has shipped with R-410A since 2010. R-410A does not deplete the ozone layer and therefore became the industry default, although it carries a high global warming potential and remains subject to future EPA regulatory review under separate rules. Continued reliance on R-22 does not offer a sustainable long-term option. The reclaimed supply will run out over time because new production remains prohibited. Homeowners who postpone replacement encounter the R-22 Phase-Out through steadily increasing prices for reclaimed refrigerant per pound, which have risen sharply since the 2020 full ban. Retrofitting an existing R-22 system to use a drop-in alternative refrigerant can sometimes be done, but a certified technician must first confirm compatibility of the compressor, metering device, and oil type before proceeding.

### R-22 phase-out compliance for Victorville property owners

R-22 Phase-Out compliance for a Victorville property owner requires knowledge of the age and refrigerant type of every cooling system on the property. Any system installed before January 1, 2010 needs inspection to verify its refrigerant. The nameplate on the outdoor unit states the refrigerant charge. A confirmed R-22 system may still be operated legally, yet adding refrigerant requires a certified technician working with reclaimed stock. The cost of repeated service often makes replacement the more practical choice within a few years. Assessment of R-22 systems involves comparing the expense of reclaimed refrigerant service, a retrofit, or complete equipment replacement against the unit’s age, overall condition, and leak record. Federal law limits who may purchase and handle refrigerant. Only certified professionals may buy refrigerant, and most refrigerant work requires EPA 608 certification.

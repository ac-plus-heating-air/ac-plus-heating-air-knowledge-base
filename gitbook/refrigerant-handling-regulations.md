### What refrigerant handling regulations are

Refrigerant Handling Regulations consist of federal Environmental Regulations administered by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. EPA Section 608 Rules cover any person who services, maintains, repairs or disposes of equipment containing refrigerant. The covered equipment includes stationary air conditioning systems, commercial refrigeration and industrial process refrigeration. The rules exist because refrigerants released into the atmosphere deplete the ozone layer and contribute to greenhouse gas accumulation. Uncontrolled release counts as a federal violation.

Refrigerant work on a [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) falls under Section 608. The same rules apply to a [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) or a [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) that uses a regulated refrigerant. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA before opening a refrigerant circuit on this equipment.

### Measurable attributes of EPA refrigerant handling regulations

EPA 608 Rules contain specific numeric requirements that define compliance. The attributes below list the most consequential thresholds a technician and equipment owner need to know.

- **EPA Section 608 certification:** Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA for any technician who purchases or handles regulated refrigerants. Four certification types exist. Type I (small appliances), Type II (high-pressure systems), Type III (low-pressure systems) and Universal. A technician must hold the type that matches the equipment being serviced.

- **Mandatory leak repair thresholds:** Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA or the local building authority regarding commercial refrigeration and air conditioning equipment with a charge above a defined threshold, leak repair timelines, and any retrofit or retirement plan that may be required.

- **Enforcement authority:** Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA or the local building authority. State agencies may impose additional requirements.

### How refrigerant handling regulations connect to the broader system

Refrigerant Handling Regulations compliance forms a required condition for legal refrigerant recovery on any air conditioning replacement or repair job. Recovery comes first. A certified technician must recover the existing charge before the old unit is removed. That recovered refrigerant must be reclaimed or properly disposed of rather than released. This requirement links the regulations directly to every stage of equipment replacement. The old system cannot be legally disconnected until recovery is complete. The new system cannot be legally charged until a certified technician performs the work.

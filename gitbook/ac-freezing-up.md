## AC freezing up in Victorville

Evaporator coil icing, known as AC freezing up, constitutes an Air Conditioner Malfunction where ice forms on the evaporator coil. The ice blocks airflow and stops the unit from transferring heat out of the building. The condition occurs when coil surface temperature falls below 32°F and causes moisture in the air to freeze on the coil fins, refrigerant lines, and cabinet. An untreated frozen AC unit ceases to cool the space, makes the compressor work against a blocked coil, and can inflict permanent damage on the refrigerant circuit.

### What AC freezing up is

AC freezing up represents a measurable failure state within the refrigeration cycle. In this state the evaporator coil surface temperature drops below 0°C and ice builds on the coil instead of condensate draining normally. During normal AC operation the coil remains cold enough to absorb heat yet warm enough for water to leave as liquid. When that balance fails, refrigerant line icing extends from the indoor coil toward the outdoor unit and the system loses cooling capacity. Confirmation requires suction pressure and coil temperature measurements taken before ice removal, a procedure a [licensed air conditioning contractor](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/air-conditioning-contractor) performs.

### Measurable attributes of a frozen AC unit

AC freezing up presents four attributes that technicians measure to verify the diagnosis and identify the root cause.

- **Evaporator coil surface temperature:** Evaporator coil surface temperature drops below 32°F during a freeze event. Normal operation keeps the coil surface between 35°F and 45°F so condensate runs off as liquid.

- **Refrigerant suction pressure:** Refrigerant suction pressure falls below 60 PSI when the system is undercharged or has a metering-device fault. Low suction pressure causes the refrigerant to expand too aggressively, pulling coil temperature below the freezing point.

- **Airflow restriction:** Airflow through the coil drops below the manufacturer's rated CFM when a clogged filter, closed register or blocked return cuts the volume of warm air reaching the coil. Without enough heat load, the coil temperature collapses.

- **Ice accumulation on coils:** Ice accumulation on coils builds in layers, starting at the coil face and spreading to the drain pan and refrigerant lines. Thick ice is insulation, compounding the temperature drop and extending recovery time after the unit is shut off.

### How AC freezing up relates to the broader cooling system

AC freezing up appears as a downstream symptom that originates from faults in other cooling system components. The evaporator coil, air filter, blower motor, metering device, and refrigerant charge interact to maintain correct coil temperature. A failure in any of these components alters the thermal balance and produces conditions for evaporator coil icing. Technicians who service a [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) trace the freeze through each component to locate the originating fault. Ductless systems follow identical physics: a [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) can develop a frozen indoor coil for identical airflow and refrigerant reasons as a ducted unit, although the ice appears on the wall-mounted air handler instead of inside a cabinet. ### Common signs of AC freezing up and not cooling

AC freezing up and not cooling ranks as the most reported symptom, yet several additional signs appear before the unit stops delivering cold air completely.

- **Warm air from supply vents:** Warm air from supply vents signals that ice has blocked enough coil surface to stop heat transfer, even though the blower continues running.

- **Ice on refrigerant lines:** Ice on the copper refrigerant lines running from the indoor unit to the outdoor condenser confirms that refrigerant line icing has extended beyond the coil.

- **Water around the air handler:** Water around the air handler appears when ice melts faster than the drain pan can carry it away, often after the thermostat cycles the system off.

- **Higher-than-normal indoor humidity:** Higher-than-normal indoor humidity develops because a frozen coil cannot dehumidify the air passing over it.

- **Unusual hissing or bubbling sounds:** Unusual hissing or bubbling sounds near the indoor unit point to refrigerant moving through a partially blocked metering device, a common trigger for low suction pressure and coil icing.

### Why is my AC freezing up: root causes

Restricted airflow and low refrigerant charge constitute the two primary causes of AC freezing. Each produces the identical outcome through a separate mechanism. A dirty air filter reduces the volume of warm air crossing the coil, so the coil absorbs less heat and surface temperature falls below 32°F. Refrigerant level matters as well. A refrigerant leak or undercharge lowers suction pressure below 60 PSI, which causes the remaining refrigerant to expand excessively and freeze the coil from the inside out. Secondary causes include a failing blower motor that cannot deliver rated CFM, a stuck or undersized expansion valve, and blocked evaporator coil fins that result from years of dust accumulation.

### How to fix AC freezing up

Correcting AC freezing up begins with complete thawing before any diagnostic measurements occur, since pressure or airflow readings taken through an ice-blocked coil yield inaccurate data. The required steps follow.

- **Shut the system off:** Shut the system off at the thermostat and switch the fan to "on" to push room-temperature air across the coil and speed the thaw. Full thaw takes two to four hours depending on ice thickness.

- **Replace or clean the air filter:** Replace or clean the air filter once the ice is gone. A filter clogged past its rated MERV capacity is the single most common cause of evaporator coil icing and the easiest fix.

- **Check all supply and return registers:** Check all supply and return registers to confirm none are closed or blocked by furniture, because even two or three closed registers can drop system airflow below the manufacturer's minimum CFM.

- **Measure suction pressure:** Measure suction pressure at the service port with a refrigerant gauge set. A reading below 60 PSI on a standard R-410A system typically indicates a refrigerant issue, which generally calls for a licensed professional to locate the leak and recharge the circuit.

- **Inspect the blower motor and coil fins:** Inspect the blower motor and coil fins for dust buildup, bent fins or a motor running below rated speed, all of which reduce airflow independently of the filter.

Evaporator coil icing that returns after a filter change indicates a refrigerant or mechanical fault that requires pressure testing and component inspection for permanent resolution.

# Gas Valve: An AC Plus Heating & Air Reference Guide

## Gas valve in Victorville

A gas valve, called a gas control valve, functions as a mechanical device that regulates the flow of natural gas or propane inside a heating system. The device opens and closes to control fuel delivery to the burner, holds safe operating pressure, and cuts off supply automatically once the system no longer demands heat. Gas valves belong to the valve family and sit alongside ball valves, gate valves and check valves. ### Parts and construction of a gas valve

A gas valve consists of several distinct components that cooperate to start, stop, and meter fuel flow. The valve body forms the outer housing, typically cast from brass, aluminum, or steel, and contains all internal parts while connecting to the gas supply line and the burner manifold. Inside the body, a ball or disc rotates or lifts against a seat seal to open or close the fuel path. The stem connects the ball or disc to the actuator. The actuator receives a signal from the thermostat or control board and drives the internal element. A damaged stem or worn seat seal counts among the most common sources of a gas valve leak.

- **Valve body:** Valve body material is brass, aluminum or steel, chosen based on the pressure rating and the installation environment.

- **Ball or disc:** Ball or disc position is binary, either fully open or fully closed, which is why gas valves are classified by their open/closed position state.

- **Seat seal:** Seat seal integrity determines whether the valve holds pressure when closed; a cracked seal allows gas to bypass the closed disc.

- **Stem:** Stem movement translates the actuator signal into physical rotation or lift of the ball or disc.

- **Actuator:** Actuator type is either manual (a lever or handle) or electromechanical (a solenoid or motor), depending on whether the valve is operator-controlled or thermostat-controlled.

Gas valves carry several rated specifications. These specifications decide whether a given valve suits a particular heating application. Size is measured in pipe diameter, typically ranging from 0.5 inches to 2 inches for residential and light commercial furnaces. Pressure rating is stated in PSI and sets the maximum pressure the valve body and seat seal can safely contain. Flow rate is measured in cubic feet per hour and must match the BTU demand of the burner; a valve with too low a flow rate starves the burner, while one rated too high can cause pressure instability.

The Cv flow coefficient is a dimensionless number, ranging from roughly 0.5 to 5.0 for residential valves, that expresses how much flow the valve passes at a given pressure differential. A higher Cv means less restriction. Technicians combine the Cv value with supply pressure and the appliance's rated input to confirm the valve will deliver the correct fuel volume. Pipe diameter and Cv together determine whether a replacement valve matches the original.

### How a gas valve fits into a heating system

A gas valve sits on the gas supply line upstream of the burner manifold inside a natural gas furnace. The valve decides whether fuel reaches the igniter and flame. The heating system relies on the valve to open on a call for heat and to close the moment the thermostat is satisfied or a safety limit trips. In a standard furnace sequence, the control board energizes the gas valve only after the inducer motor has proven airflow and the igniter has reached temperature, placing the valve as the final control point before combustion begins.

Gas valves appear in natural gas furnaces, propane furnaces and some boiler systems. They do not appear in electric furnaces, [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) systems or [ductless mini split systems](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system), which transfer heat electrically and contain no fuel gas line. A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) paired with a gas furnace shares the same air handler and ductwork as the furnace, yet the gas valve belongs exclusively to the heating side. Knowledge of which components belong to which system lets a technician locate a no-heat fault quickly.

### Gas valve vs. Related components

A gas valve differs from a gas regulator. A regulator lowers supply pressure from the utility line to the appliance operating pressure, typically 3.5 inches of water column for natural gas, and maintains that pressure steady regardless of flow demand. The gas valve sits downstream of the regulator. The valve opens or closes on command and performs no pressure reduction or stabilization itself. Mixing up the two produces misdiagnosis: a failed regulator creates low-pressure symptoms on every appliance on the line, while a failed gas valve affects only the single appliance it serves.

A pilot valve is a smaller dedicated valve that controls gas flow to the standing pilot flame in older furnaces. Modern furnaces employ electronic ignition instead. They depend on a single main gas valve rather than a separate pilot valve. An electric valve employs a solenoid coil to open and close electromagnetically; most modern residential gas control valves operate as electric valves energized by 24-volt signals from the control board. When a fuel gas valve fails in the open position or develops a crack in the body, the outcome is uncontrolled gas flow. Technicians refer to this condition as a gas valve leak.

### Common problems and signs of a failing gas valve

Gas valve problems fall into two categories: valves that fail closed and valves that fail open or leak. Failure closed creates a no-heat call. The furnace proceeds through inducer and igniter stages, but the burner never lights because no fuel reaches the manifold. A valve that fails open or develops a seat seal crack produces a gas valve leak, identified by the smell of mercaptan near the furnace or by a combustible gas detector reading above zero at the valve body.

Additional signs include a valve that opens intermittently, so the furnace lights on some calls for heat and not on others, and a valve whose actuator coil has failed, leaving it unresponsive to the 24-volt signal from the control board. Technicians verify a failed coil by measuring resistance across the coil terminals. An open circuit reading indicates the coil is burned out. The fuel gas valve cannot be serviced in the field. When it fails, the correct action is complete replacement with a valve that matches size, pipe diameter, pressure rating, and Cv coefficient.

### Adjust gas valve settings and when to call a technician

To adjust gas valve outlet pressure, a technician removes the pressure port cap on the valve body, attaches a manometer to the port, and turns the adjustment screw until the reading matches the appliance manufacturer's specification, typically 3.5 inches of water column for natural gas or 10 inches for propane. This procedure is not a homeowner task. Gas pressure outside the rated range produces incomplete combustion, carbon monoxide production or burner damage. Gas pressure checks and adjustments are performed by an [HVAC contractor](air-conditioning-contractor.md), often during a furnace tune-up or after any valve replacement. Homeowners who smell gas near the furnace must turn off the manual gas cock, exit the building and call the gas utility before contacting a heating contractor.

## Duct sealing in Victorville - AC Plus Heating & Air

Duct sealing, called HVAC Duct Sealing, is a type of HVAC System Efficiency Service that closes gaps, cracks and disconnected joints in a home's ductwork to stop conditioned air from escaping before it reaches the living space. The process covers supply ducts, return ducts and the plenums that connect them to the air handler, using materials such as mastic sealant, metallic foil tape and aerosol-based sealant injection to restore airtightness across the full duct system. Unsealed ductwork in a typical home loses 10 to 30 percent of conditioned air to unconditioned spaces like attics and crawlspaces, which means the HVAC system works harder, runs longer and still delivers uneven temperatures. Air Duct Sealing directly enhances HVAC system performance and air conditioning efficiency, making it a high-return efficiency service a Victorville homeowner can schedule.

### What duct sealing is and how it works

Duct sealing is the diagnosis and physical closure of air leakage points in a duct system so that conditioned air travels only where it is intended. A technician first performs a pressure test, measuring airflow in cubic feet per minute at 25 pascals of pressure (CFM25) before any work begins. The system is then re-tested after sealing to confirm the leakage rate has dropped. That gap between the two CFM25 readings is measurable proof. [AC Plus HVAC](https://share.google/7avxFb2OAOZM1JYKh) performs this service for homes throughout Victorville, where attic temperatures regularly exceed 140°F in summer and accelerate duct joint failure.

Three sealant material types are used depending on duct system type and access. **Mastic sealant** is a water-based compound brushed directly onto joints and seams; it remains flexible after curing and bonds to sheet metal, fiberglass duct board and flexible duct. **Metallic foil tape** reinforces mastic on rigid duct connections and is rated for high-temperature environments. **Aerosol-based sealant injection** (sometimes called Aeroseal) pressurizes the duct system and deposits sealant particles from the inside, reaching leaks that are physically inaccessible from the exterior. Each method targets a different duct system type, and a professional duct sealing service selects the right approach after the initial pressure test.

### Measurable attributes of duct sealing

Duct sealing produces results that are testable before and after the job, not estimated. The attributes below define what a completed service delivers.

- **Air leakage reduction potential:** Air leakage reduction potential ranges from 5 to 30 percent of total conditioned air, depending on the age of the duct system and how many joints were originally left unsealed.

- **Leakage rate (CFM25):** Leakage rate is measured in cubic feet per minute at 25 pascals; a well-sealed residential system targets a post-service CFM25 reading that meets or falls below local energy code thresholds.

- **Airtightness (Pa):** Airtightness is expressed in pascals and confirms how much pressure the sealed duct system holds; a higher post-seal pressure reading means fewer remaining leaks.

- **Sealant material type:** Sealant material type determines durability and temperature rating; mastic and metallic foil tape are the standard pairing for rigid duct, while aerosol injection handles inaccessible flexible duct runs.

- **Duct system type:** Duct system type (rigid sheet metal, fiberglass duct board or flexible duct) governs which sealant materials and application methods are appropriate for the job.

- **Pressure test rating:** Pressure test rating records the CFM25 value pre- and post-service, giving the homeowner a documented before-and-after comparison of leakage reduction.

### How duct sealing relates to the broader HVAC system

Ductwork Sealing sits at the intersection of the air distribution system and every piece of equipment connected to it. A sealed duct system reduces the static pressure the air handler works against, which lowers motor amperage draw and extends blower life. It protects the heat exchanger in a furnace and the evaporator coil in a [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) from the strain of restricted airflow caused by pressure imbalances. Duct sealing and insulation are often scheduled together because sealing stops air loss while insulation stops heat transfer through the duct wall itself; the two services address different failure modes in the same component.

A [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) bypasses this problem entirely by delivering conditioned air directly to each zone with no duct runs. That contrast makes duct sealing most relevant to homes with existing forced-air systems, where the duct network is already in place and leakage is the primary efficiency loss. An [air conditioning contractor](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/air-conditioning-contractor) can assess whether sealing the existing ducts or converting to a ductless system produces the better long-term outcome for a specific home layout.

### Signs of duct leakage and common problem areas

Duct leakage shows up as a pattern of symptoms. No single obvious failure signals the problem. Rooms that are consistently warmer or cooler than the thermostat setting, high utility bills that do not match usage patterns, and visible dust accumulation near supply registers are the most common indicators. Flexible duct connections at the air handler and at each register boot are high-failure points, because the clamps and tape used during original installation degrade over time, especially in attics where thermal cycling is extreme.

Unsealed ductwork pulls unconditioned air into the return side of the system. Return-side leaks draw attic air, crawlspace air, and garage fumes into the air stream, reducing indoor air quality and forcing the equipment to condition air that was never meant to enter the system. Supply-side airflow loss drops room pressure. That pressure drop causes doors to slam and pulls outdoor air through wall gaps. Both problems are addressed when the duct system is properly sealed and pressure-tested to confirm the fix.

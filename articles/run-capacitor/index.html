A run capacitor. Called an AC capacitor or motor run capacitor. Is an electrical component that stores and discharges energy continuously to keep AC motors running through every cooling cycle. It powers the compressor, the condenser fan motor, and the blower motor in a central air conditioning system. Run capacitors are built from conductor plates separated by a dielectric film, housed in an aluminum casing with terminal leads. Without one, an AC motor cannot sustain operation after startup.

What a run capacitor is and how it works

A run capacitor sustains motor rotation after startup. It stores an electrical charge and releases it in a precise, phase-shifted wave that keeps the motor's windings energized in the correct sequence. The result is smooth, continuous rotation. Not the stuttering current draw a motor produces on single-phase power alone.

Run capacitors are rated by two measurable values: capacitance in microfarads (µF) and voltage in volts AC (VAC). A capacitor outside its rated range delivers the wrong phase shift, and the motor runs hot, draws excess current or fails to start. Measurable attributes of a run capacitor

Run capacitors carry three attributes that determine whether a replacement part is correct for a given motor.

  • Capacitance: Capacitance is measured in microfarads and typically falls between 5 µF and 80 µF for residential AC motors. The compressor capacitor usually sits at the higher end of that range; fan motor capacitors are often 5 µF to 15 µF.
  • Voltage rating: Voltage rating is expressed in VAC and is almost always 370V or 440V for residential and light-commercial equipment. A 440V capacitor works safely on a 370V circuit; the reverse is not true and causes premature failure.
  • Operating temperature range: The operating temperature range for a standard run capacitor runs from -40°C to +70°C. Victorville summers push outdoor unit temperatures well above 50°C, so a capacitor already weakened by age reaches its thermal limit faster than it does in a cooler climate.

Capacitance tolerance matters too. A capacitor that reads more than 6% below its rated µF value on a multimeter is considered out of tolerance and should be replaced, even if the motor is still running.

How a run capacitor relates to the AC system

A run capacitor enables continuous operation of the air conditioner motor throughout the cooling cycle. In a typical split system, a dual run capacitor serves both the compressor and the condenser fan motor from a single cylindrical or oval can. It has three terminals. These are labeled C (common), HERM (compressor), and FAN. Each winding carries its own µF rating printed on the label. For example, 45/5 µF, meaning 45 µF to the compressor and 5 µF to the fan.

The run capacitor is distinct from the start capacitor. A start capacitor provides a large, brief surge of energy only during the first fraction of a second of motor startup, then disconnects from the circuit. A run capacitor stays in the circuit the entire time the motor runs. Confusing the two leads to incorrect replacements: installing a start capacitor in a run position causes rapid overheating because start capacitors are not rated for continuous duty.

Common problems and signs of a failing run capacitor

Run capacitor failure is one of the most frequent causes of a no-cool call in the Victorville area, where high ambient temperatures accelerate capacitor aging. A failing motor run capacitor shows several recognizable signs before it fails completely.

  • Hard starting: Hard starting happens when the compressor or fan motor takes several seconds to reach full speed, sometimes with a humming sound, because the capacitor can no longer deliver enough phase-shifted current.
  • Warm air from the vents: Warm air from the vents with the system running often means the compressor is drawing power but not building pressure, a sign the compressor capacitor has dropped below its rated µF.
  • Fan not spinning: A condenser fan that sits still while the compressor runs points directly to a failed FAN side of a dual run capacitor.
  • Swollen or leaking casing: A swollen top or oily residue on the aluminum casing is a visual sign of internal dielectric breakdown. Replace the capacitor immediately.
  • High energy bills: A motor running without proper capacitor support draws more current than its nameplate rating, which shows up as higher electricity use before any other symptom appears.

A technician confirms the diagnosis with a capacitor meter. Reading capacitance live, under load, is more reliable than a static multimeter test because a capacitor can test within tolerance cold and still fail at operating temperature. Run capacitor replacement for AC units in Victorville

Run capacitor replacement is a straightforward repair when the correct part is on hand, but the stored charge in a capacitor can reach several hundred volts even after the unit is powered off. A technician discharges the capacitor through a resistor before touching the terminals. Skipping that step risks serious injury during capacitor replacement.

Three numbers matter. Matching a replacement capacitor requires the µF rating, the VAC rating, and the physical size or terminal configuration from the label on the old unit. Substituting a higher µF value than specified overloads the motor windings; substituting a lower value produces the same hard-start and overheating symptoms the failed capacitor caused. The voltage rating of the replacement must meet or exceed the original. Never fall below it.

A run capacitor for an AC unit in the Victorville climate should be inspected every cooling season. Capacitors degrade gradually, losing capacitance each year. A unit running on a weak capacitor draws excess current, shortening compressor life. Catching a capacitor at 80% of rated capacitance during a maintenance visit costs far less than a compressor replacement after the motor burns out.

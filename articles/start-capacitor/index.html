A start capacitor, called a Motor Start Capacitor, is a type of electrical capacitor that delivers a short, high-energy current boost to an AC motor at the moment it starts, providing the phase-shifted torque needed to overcome the motor's initial inertia and bring it up to operating speed. Start capacitors are found in air conditioning compressors, refrigeration units, heat pump compressors and other single-phase induction motors that cannot generate enough starting torque on their own. The component is an electrolytic design built around an aluminum cylinder, terminal leads and an insulating sleeve, and it disconnects from the circuit within a fraction of a second once the motor reaches roughly 75 percent of its rated speed.

What a start capacitor is

A start capacitor is an electrolytic capacitor wired in series with the start winding of a single-phase AC motor to create a phase shift between the run winding and start winding currents. That phase difference produces a rotating magnetic field strong enough to spin the rotor from a dead stop. Once the motor is running, a centrifugal switch or a run capacitor circuit removes the starting capacitor from the loop, because leaving it energized will overheat and destroy it within seconds. The start cap is therefore an intermittent-duty device, rated for brief on-cycles rather than continuous operation.

Measurable attributes of a start capacitor

Start capacitors carry two critical ratings stamped on the body: capacitance in microfarads (µF) and voltage in volts AC (VAC). Capacitance matters. A typical residential AC start capacitor runs from 88 µF to 108 µF, though motor-specific values outside that range exist for larger or specialty equipment. Voltage ratings are commonly 125 VAC or 330 VAC, and the installed unit must meet or exceed the motor's rated voltage or the capacitor fails prematurely. Discharge time is a third measurable attribute: a start capacitor stores charge and must bleed that charge down to a safe level within a few seconds of the motor reaching speed, which is why the switching mechanism that removes it from the circuit is so important. Selecting a replacement requires matching all three values to the original specification, not one alone.

How a start capacitor relates to the AC system

Inside the electrical system of an air conditioning compressor, a start capacitor enables the compressor motor to start under load. Without it, the compressor draws locked-rotor amperage far beyond its rated current, trips the breaker, or simply hums and stalls. The start capacitor works alongside the run capacitor, but the two serve different functions: the run capacitor stays in the circuit continuously to improve motor efficiency and power factor, while the starting capacitor is only active for the first fraction of a second of each start cycle. A failed start cap leaves the run capacitor unable to compensate, so the compressor motor cannot start at all. That relationship makes the start capacitor one of the first components a technician checks when a compressor hums but does not turn over.

Signs of a bad start capacitor

A bad start capacitor produces a recognizable set of symptoms in an AC system. The compressor attempts to start, draws high amperage for two to three seconds, then trips the breaker or shuts down on thermal overload. A humming or clicking sound from the outdoor unit with no compressor rotation is the most common field sign. Capacitance loss is measurable with a capacitor meter. A unit reading more than 6 percent below its rated µF value is considered out of tolerance and warrants start capacitor replacement. Physical signs include a bulged or cracked cap on the capacitor body, oil leakage from the casing, or burn marks on the terminals. AC start capacitor replacement and service

AC start capacitor replacement is a repair that requires discharging the stored voltage before any handling, because a charged capacitor can deliver a dangerous shock even with the unit powered off. A technician measures the capacitance and voltage rating of the failed unit, sources a direct replacement matching those specifications, and tests the motor's start winding resistance before installing the new part to rule out a winding fault that could destroy the replacement immediately.

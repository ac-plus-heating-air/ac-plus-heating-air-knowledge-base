R-32 Refrigerant, known as HFC-32 or difluoromethane, is a single-component hydrofluorocarbon refrigerant with the chemical formula CH2F2, a molecular weight of 52.02 g/mol, a boiling point of -51.7°C at standard atmospheric pressure, a Global Warming Potential of 675, and a flammability classification of A2L, making it one of the more widely adopted modern refrigerants in residential and commercial air conditioning systems across the United States and globally.

R-32 Refrigerant is a type of refrigerant, sitting within the broader HFC refrigerant family alongside compounds used in split systems, heat pumps and ductless equipment. AC Plus Heating and Air works with R-32 refrigerant AC units throughout Victorville and the surrounding High Desert region.

What R-32 refrigerant is and how it differs from R-22 and R-410A

R-32 is a pure, single-component HFC refrigerant. It is not a blend. Its chemical formula, CH2F2, contains only carbon, hydrogen, and fluorine atoms. That single-component structure makes it easier to recover, reclaim, and recharge in the field compared to blended refrigerants, because the ratio of components does not shift during a leak the way it does in a mixture.

R-32 Refrigerant stands in direct contrast to R-22 Refrigerant and R-410A Refrigerant, the two refrigerants it is steadily replacing. R-22 carries a GWP of approximately 1,810 and is now banned from production and import in the United States under the Clean Air Act phaseout. R-410A carries a GWP of 2,088, more than three times higher than R-32's GWP of 675. Switching from R-410A to R-32 refrigerant gas cuts the direct climate impact of the refrigerant charge by roughly 68 percent. An air conditioning contractor familiar with A2L-rated refrigerants is required for safe handling, since R-32 carries a mild flammability rating that older R-22 and R-410A systems were not designed around.

Measurable attributes of R-32 refrigerant

R-32 Refrigerant has a defined set of physical and environmental attributes that determine where and how it is used. The attributes below are the ones technicians and equipment manufacturers rely on when specifying or servicing an R-32 refrigerant system installation.

  • Global Warming Potential (GWP): Global Warming Potential for R-32 is 675, measured over a 100-year horizon. That figure is the primary reason manufacturers shifted away from R-410A, whose GWP of 2,088 triggered regulatory pressure in the European Union and the United States.
  • Boiling point: Boiling point for difluoromethane is -51.7°C at one atmosphere of pressure. That low boiling point allows R-32 to absorb heat efficiently at the evaporator coil, which is the core mechanism behind cooling in a split or ducted system.
  • Molecular weight: Molecular weight of CH2F2 is 52.02 g/mol, lighter than R-410A at 72.6 g/mol. The lower molecular weight means a smaller refrigerant charge is needed to achieve the same cooling capacity, which reduces the total amount of refrigerant in the system.
  • Flammability class: Flammability class for R-32 is A2L under ASHRAE Standard 34, meaning it is mildly flammable with a low burning velocity. Equipment designed for A2L refrigerants includes safety features such as leak detection and ventilation requirements not present in older R-22 or R-410A units.
  • Chemical formula: Chemical formula is CH2F2, a simple two-carbon-free structure with one carbon atom bonded to two hydrogen and two fluorine atoms. That simplicity is why R-32 behaves as a pure substance rather than a zeotropic blend.

How R-32 refrigerant relates to air conditioning systems

R-32 Refrigerant is used in air conditioning systems as the working fluid that moves heat from inside a building to the outdoor unit. In a central air conditioning system, difluoromethane circulates through the compressor, condenser coil, expansion valve and evaporator coil in a continuous loop, absorbing heat indoors and releasing it outside. The same refrigerant cycle applies in a ductless mini split system, where R-32 is now the standard charge in most new inverter-driven units sold in the United States.

R-32 Refrigerant is used in heat pump systems, where the refrigerant cycle reverses direction to provide heating in winter and cooling in summer. Because R-32 performs efficiently across a wide range of operating temperatures, it suits the variable-speed compressors common in modern heat pump equipment. Manufacturers including Daikin, Mitsubishi and Fujitsu have standardized on HFC-32 across their residential product lines for this reason.

Signs of an R-32 refrigerant problem in a system

R-32 refrigerant problems in an AC unit typically show up as reduced cooling output, ice forming on the evaporator coil, or the system running continuously without reaching the set temperature. Refrigerant does not get consumed. It circulates in a sealed loop, which means a low charge is almost always the result of a leak rather than normal consumption. If the level drops, a leak exists somewhere in that loop.

Three field indicators point to an R-32 refrigerant gas leak: a hissing sound near the indoor or outdoor unit, oily residue on refrigerant line connections, and a noticeable drop in airflow temperature at the supply registers. R-32 carries an A2L flammability rating. A leak in an enclosed mechanical room or tight equipment closet warrants prompt attention for that reason. Refrigerant work on R-32 systems requires EPA Section 608-certified handling by a licensed technician, so a homeowner should not attempt to add or recover the charge without proper equipment and certification.

CSLB contractor license in Victorville

A CSLB Contractor License, called a California Contractor's License, is a type of Professional Licensure issued by the California Contractors State License Board. The license identifies the holder by a unique alphanumeric license number, assigns one or more trade classifications, and requires a surety bond and proof of workers' compensation insurance before the Board activates it.

CSLB Contractor License requirements

CSLB Contractor License requirements center on four components: a qualifying individual, a surety bond, workers' compensation insurance and a passing score on the trade and law examinations. The qualifying individual is the person whose experience and examination results back the license.

Every CSLB License carries a set of measurable attributes the Board records and publishes in its public database. The license number is a unique alphanumeric identifier assigned at issuance. AC Plus Heating & Air holds CSLB License #805216. Consumers can verify all of these attributes in real time through the Board's online lookup tool.

How to check CSLB Contractor License status

To check CSLB Contractor License status, a homeowner or project owner enters the contractor's license number or business name into the Board's public license lookup at www.cslb.ca.gov. The result shows the license number, the holder's legal business name, the active or inactive status, the expiration date, the bond on file, any workers' compensation exemption or policy number, and any disciplinary actions or citations on record. Checking status before signing a contract is the single most reliable way to confirm a contractor is legally authorized to do the work. A ductless mini split system installation and a heat pump replacement both fall under the C-20 classification, so the status check should confirm that classification is listed alongside the active status before work begins.

Unlicensed operation and the Owner-Builder Exemption

Unlicensed operation is the direct contrast to holding a valid California Contractor's License. The consequences are significant. The Owner-Builder Exemption is a narrow carve-out that allows a property owner to pull permits and act as their own general contractor on a property they own and intend to occupy. It does not authorize an unlicensed third party to perform the work, and it does not apply to commercial properties.

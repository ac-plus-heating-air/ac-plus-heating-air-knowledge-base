System Startup and Commissioning, called AC commissioning, is the structured verification process that confirms a newly installed or replaced air conditioning system operates to its design specifications. It covers refrigerant pressure testing, thermostat calibration, airflow balancing, electrical load checks, and a full functional test before handover to the homeowner. It is a type of HVAC Installation Completion Process. This places it at the final stage of any AC installation rather than as a standalone service call. The HVAC startup process cannot begin until refrigerant charging, electrical connections, and thermostat wiring are all complete. AC Plus performs this procedure on every installation in Victorville, CA to confirm the system delivers the performance the equipment is rated for.

What system startup and commissioning is

System Startup and Commissioning is the formal sequence of pre-startup checks, measurements, and adjustments that transforms a mechanically installed AC unit into a verified, operating system. The distinction from a routine service visit is precise. Every reading is compared against the manufacturer's design specifications, not a general industry average. A startup and commissioning report documents each measurement, giving both the homeowner and the installing contractor a written record of the system's baseline performance on day one.

System Activation is the moment the unit is energized for the first time under load, but that single event is only one step inside the broader commissioning sequence. Skipping the surrounding verification steps leaves refrigerant charge, airflow and electrical load unconfirmed, which shortens equipment life and voids most manufacturer warranties. A licensed air conditioning contractor is required to perform refrigerant work under EPA 608 rules, so commissioning is not a task a homeowner can complete independently.

Measurable attributes of AC system startup and commissioning

AC system startup and commissioning produces a set of recorded measurements that define the system's verified baseline. Each attribute below has a specific target range; a reading outside that range triggers an adjustment before the report is signed off.

  • Startup duration: Startup duration runs 1 to 4 hours depending on system type, zone count and site conditions.
  • Refrigerant charge verification: Refrigerant charge verification holds a tolerance of plus or minus 0.1 oz to match the manufacturer's specified charge weight exactly.
  • Airflow measurement: Airflow measurement records cubic feet per minute (CFM) at each supply and return register to confirm balanced delivery across every zone.
  • System pressure readings: System pressure readings, taken in psi on both the high and low sides, confirm the refrigerant circuit is sealed and charged correctly.
  • Electrical load: Electrical load is measured in amps at startup and compared against the nameplate rating to catch wiring faults or undersized breakers before they cause a failure.
  • Temperature stability: Temperature stability is verified in degrees Celsius across supply and return air to confirm the system is achieving its rated delta-T.
  • Energy efficiency: Energy efficiency, expressed in kWh draw under full load, is logged at commissioning so any future increase signals a developing fault.

A central air conditioning system typically requires all seven measurements above. A ductless mini split system follows the same refrigerant and electrical checks but replaces duct CFM measurement with individual air handler output readings at each indoor head.

How system startup and commissioning relates to the broader installation process

System Startup and Commissioning sits at the end of the HVAC Installation Completion Process and cannot begin until three prior steps are finished: refrigerant charging, electrical connection and thermostat calibration. Those three steps are components of the installation sequence and must be confirmed complete before the system startup procedure moves into functional testing under real load conditions.

A heat pump installation adds a reversing-valve check to the standard commissioning sequence because the unit must be verified in both heating and cooling modes. The relationship between commissioning and the broader installation is sequential and dependent: commissioning is the gate the system must pass before the warranty clock starts and before the homeowner accepts the equipment.

Common problems found during the HVAC startup process

The HVAC startup process is specifically designed to surface faults that visual inspection misses. Refrigerant undercharge is the most common finding. It often occurs by a fraction of an ounce that goes undetected without the plus-or-minus 0.1 oz verification step. Low airflow at one or more registers points to a duct connection that was not fully seated during installation. An electrical load reading above the nameplate amperage signals a wiring fault or a breaker that is too small for the equipment.

System Startup and Commissioning is the direct contrast to system decommissioning, which is the controlled shutdown and isolation of a system at end of life or before major repair. It differs from an emergency shutdown. That is an unplanned stop triggered by a safety fault. A system malfunction after handover. Such as a refrigerant leak or compressor failure. Is evidence that commissioning either was not performed or that a fault developed after the verified baseline was established. Catching these issues at startup, rather than after the homeowner moves in, prevents callback visits and supports the equipment's continued reliable operation.

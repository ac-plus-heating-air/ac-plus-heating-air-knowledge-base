## Limit switch in Victorville

A limit switch, called a Temperature Limit Switch, is a temperature-activated safety switch that cuts power to a furnace burner when the heat exchanger reaches a dangerously high temperature, protecting the system from overheating, cracked heat exchangers, fire risk and carbon monoxide exposure. ### Parts that make up a limit switch

A limit switch is built from a bimetallic disc or bimetal strip that flexes when temperature rises, a set of electrical contacts that open or close in response, a mounting bracket that fixes the switch to the furnace plenum, a protective housing, an actuator that transmits disc movement to the contacts, and the wiring that carries the signal to the control board. The bimetal strip is the active element. It bends at a calibrated temperature and either breaks or completes the circuit depending on the switch type. Most residential units use a normally closed contact arrangement, meaning the circuit stays closed during normal operation and opens only when the temperature limit is exceeded.

### Measurable attributes of a limit switch

Limit switches carry several measurable specifications that determine where and how they can be used. The five most important are listed below.

- **Activation temperature:** Activation temperature is the point at which the switch trips, typically set between 30°C and 90°C (roughly 86°F to 194°F) for residential furnace applications, with the exact value stamped on the switch body.

- **Electrical rating:** Electrical rating defines the maximum voltage and current the contacts can safely carry. Most furnace limit switches are rated at 24 volts and 5 to 10 amps, matching the low-voltage control circuit of a standard gas furnace.

- **Switch type:** Switch type describes the contact configuration. SPST (single-pole, single-throw) is the most common, meaning one circuit is either open or closed, with no intermediate state.

- **Temperature range:** Temperature range is the full span of temperatures the switch can withstand without damage, expressed in both Fahrenheit and Celsius on the manufacturer label.

- **Size and material type:** Size is measured in inches across the disc or housing diameter, and material type refers to the housing and contact materials. Typically a high-temperature polymer housing with copper or silver-alloy contacts that resist oxidation at elevated temperatures.

### How a limit switch fits into the furnace safety system

A limit switch is a type of safety switch. It sits inside a broader furnace safety control system alongside the pressure switch, rollout switch, and flame sensor. Its specific role is high limit protection. The last electrical barrier before an overheating furnace causes structural damage. When the plenum temperature climbs above the set point, the switch opens the low-voltage control circuit, shutting off the gas valve and burner within seconds. The blower motor often continues running after a trip to exhaust residual heat, which is normal behavior controlled by the fan limit function built into many combination units.

A [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) uses a different set of safety controls, refrigerant pressure switches and thermal overloads, because it manages refrigerant pressure rather than combustion heat. A [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) in heating mode relies on refrigerant-side protection rather than a high limit switch, since it does not burn fuel. The limit switch is specific to fuel-burning heating equipment: gas furnaces, oil furnaces and some electric furnaces with resistance heating elements.

### Limit switch vs. Thermostat and pressure switch

A limit switch is not a thermostat and is not a pressure switch, though all three affect furnace operation. A thermostat measures room air temperature and signals the furnace to start or stop a heating cycle based on comfort settings. It is a demand control, not a safety device. A pressure switch monitors negative pressure in the flue or inducer to confirm safe venting before allowing ignition. The High Limit Switch monitors plenum temperature. It acts only when something has already gone wrong. A blocked filter, a failed blower motor, or a collapsed duct restricting airflow. Where the thermostat governs comfort and the pressure switch governs venting, the limit switch governs heat exchanger protection.

A [ductless mini-split air conditioner](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-air-conditioner) has no plenum and no combustion, so it uses neither a high limit switch nor a pressure switch in the furnace sense. Its safety controls are refrigerant-side and electronic. ### Common problems and signs a limit switch needs attention

A furnace limit switch fails in two ways: it trips repeatedly during normal operation, or it fails to trip when it should. Repeated tripping is rarely a switch problem. When the furnace shuts off after a short run and restarts only after cooling, the cause is almost always restricted airflow rather than a defective switch. A clogged air filter is the most common cause, followed by blocked return vents or a failing blower motor. Replacing the switch without fixing the airflow problem brings the furnace back online for only a short time before the new switch trips again.

A switch that fails to open when the plenum overheats is the more dangerous failure mode. The furnace continues running past its safe temperature limit, stressing the heat exchanger and raising the risk of a crack that allows combustion gases into the living space. That risk is serious. Signs that a limit switch may have failed open include a furnace that runs continuously without cycling off, a heat exchanger that is visibly discolored or warped, or a control board fault code pointing to a limit circuit fault.

To reset limit switch function after a nuisance trip, a technician first identifies and corrects the root cause, typically replacing the air filter and verifying blower operation, then manually resets the switch if it is the manual-reset type, or allows it to cool and reset automatically if it is the auto-reset type. To replace limit switch components, the technician matches the replacement part to the original activation temperature, electrical rating and switch type stamped on the old unit, since installing a switch with a higher trip point than the furnace manufacturer specifies defeats the protection the switch is designed to provide.

### Where limit switches apply across HVAC equipment

Limit switches appear in gas furnaces, oil furnaces, electric furnaces with resistance elements, rooftop package units and some light commercial heating systems. In a gas furnace the Fan Limit Switch often combines the high limit function with fan-on and fan-off temperature control in a single device, so replacing it requires matching all three set points in addition to the high limit value. In rooftop units the limit switch protects the heat exchanger in the heating section while a separate set of refrigerant-side controls protects the cooling section.

## Reversing valve in Victorville

A reversing valve, called a changeover valve, is an electromechanical HVAC component that directs refrigerant flow through a heat pump system, switching the circuit between heating mode and cooling mode without changing the compressor's direction of rotation. The valve is a type of HVAC component found exclusively in [heat pump](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/heat-pump) systems, where the ability to reverse refrigerant flow is what separates a heat pump from a standard air conditioner. ### Component parts and physical design of a reversing valve

A reversing valve is built around a four-way spool design. The valve body contains four refrigerant ports and an internal slide mechanism that repositions to connect those ports in two different configurations. The main component parts are the solenoid coil, the pilot valve, the slide piston, the slide mechanism, and the main valve body. The solenoid coil receives a 24V AC signal from the thermostat, which energizes the pilot valve and shifts a small pressure differential that moves the slide piston inside the main valve body. One piston position routes refrigerant through the heating-mode circuit; the other reverses the circuit for cooling. The four-way valve is distinct from an expansion valve, which meters refrigerant volume and pressure, and from a check valve, which permits flow in only one direction. Neither of those components can reverse a refrigerant circuit.

Reversing valves carry several measurable attributes that determine compatibility with a given heat pump. Operating voltage is 24V AC. This voltage is supplied by the low-voltage control circuit. The valve has four ports. One each for the compressor discharge line, the compressor suction line, the indoor coil, and the outdoor coil. Operating pressure range runs from roughly 150 to 450 PSI, depending on the refrigerant in use. Modern residential heat pumps commonly use R-410A refrigerant, and the valve body must be rated for that refrigerant's higher operating pressures. Operating temperature range varies by manufacturer, but the valve must tolerate the full span of conditions the heat pump encounters, from sub-freezing outdoor temperatures in heating mode to high-ambient cooling conditions in summer. A valve selected outside its rated pressure or temperature range will fail prematurely, making specification matching a required step during any reversing valve repair or replacement.

### How the reversing valve fits into the heat pump system

The reversing valve is a part of the heat pump system and sits in the refrigerant circuit between the compressor and the two coils. In a [central air conditioning system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/central-air-conditioning-system) that does not include a heat pump, no reversing valve is present. The refrigerant circuit runs in one fixed direction and the system can only remove heat from the indoor air. Adding a reversing valve is what converts the refrigeration cycle into a bidirectional heat pump cycle. In a [ductless mini split system](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/ductless-mini-split-system) with heating capability, the reversing valve performs the same function in a compact refrigerant circuit without ductwork. The thermostat controls the solenoid coil directly: in most designs, the coil is energized in cooling mode and de-energized in heating mode, though some manufacturers reverse this convention, which matters when diagnosing a stuck valve.

### Common reversing valve failure signs and diagnosis

Reversing valve failure typically shows up as a heat pump that blows warm air in cooling mode, cold air in heating mode, or air that is neither warm nor cool regardless of the thermostat setting. A reversing valve stuck in one position is the most frequent failure mode: the slide piston seizes due to pressure imbalance, wear or debris in the refrigerant circuit, leaving the system locked in heating or cooling regardless of the thermostat command. A failed solenoid coil is a separate failure mode where the valve body itself is intact but the electrical signal never reaches the pilot valve, so the slide never shifts. Technicians distinguish between these two by checking for 24V AC at the solenoid terminals while the thermostat calls for a mode change: voltage present with no valve movement points to a mechanical slide failure; no voltage points to a wiring or control board fault. Refrigerant leaking from the valve body is a third failure mode and requires full valve replacement rather than repair, because the brazed connections and internal seals are not serviceable in the field. An [air conditioning contractor](https://ac-plus-heating-air.mintlify.site/air-conditioning-contractor) with heat pump experience can confirm which failure mode is present before ordering parts, avoiding an unnecessary compressor replacement.

### Reversing valve repair and replacement considerations

Reversing valve repair in most field situations means full valve replacement. The internal slide and pilot valve are not sold as separate service parts by most manufacturers. Replacement requires recovering the refrigerant charge, unbrazing the four refrigerant line connections, installing the new valve body, rebrazing, pressure-testing, and recharging the system. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the EPA. Selecting the correct replacement valve requires matching the original valve's refrigerant type, operating pressure range, and port configuration. Specifications matter. An R-410A-rated four-way valve cannot be substituted with an older R-22-rated unit even if the physical dimensions appear similar. After installation, the technician verifies mode-switching function by cycling the thermostat between heating and cooling and confirming that suction and discharge line temperatures shift as expected within a few minutes of each mode change.
